**Quick answer:** Saturn in the 7th house (*Jamitra*, the house of marriage and partnership) means a good spouse will not feel like one, and friendship comes up just as empty. The classical text ties that isolation directly to unstable wealth and to chronic health trouble shared by both partners, and names the psychological cause: an agitated mind that fails before circumstance even gets the chance to.

## A Good Spouse, Undelivered

The 7th is a *Kendrasthana*, the point of union directly opposite the *Lagna*, known in the classical lexicon as *Dyuna*, the setting-sun house, *Jamitra*, and *Kalabrabhaya*, the primary significator of the spouse (*Brihat Jataka*, Ch. I; *Phaladeepika*, Ch. 10). The source for Saturn's results here is the *Chamatkara Chintaamani* (Shani Phalam, Verse 7): "Sudārā na mitraṃ ciraṃ cāru vittaṃ śanau dyūnage dampatī rogayuktau, anutsāhasantaptakṛd hīnacetāḥ kuto vīryavān vihvalo lolupaḥ syāt." *Sudārā na mitraṃ*: even where the spouse is genuinely good (*Sudārā*), the native does not receive the comfort or benefit of that union. The same verse closes off friendship entirely, *na mitraṃ*. The problem here is not the quality of the people around the native. It is the native's inability to draw support from people who are, by the text's own account, worth having.

## No Allies, So No Lasting Wealth Either

The verse's logic runs in sequence: *chiraṃ cāru vittaṃ* denied, wealth that is neither long-lasting nor substantial. In a social structure where material success ran through alliances, the absence of *mitra* is the precondition for the absence of durable wealth. Without a circle of support, professional effort stays solitary, and whatever is gained is prone to being transient or unsatisfying even when it arrives.

## Health and Temperament Under Strain

*Dampatī rogayuktau*: both the native and the spouse are subject to chronic disease, a shared physical burden rather than one partner's alone. The psychological picture is just as unflinching. The native is *anutsāhasantaptakṛd*, distressed and without enthusiasm, and *hīnacetāḥ*, small-minded, lacking any noble aspiration. The verse asks outright, *kuto vīryavān*, where is the valor, a rhetorical admission that there is none. The native is *vihvalo*, overwhelmed, and *lolupaḥ*, restless with a greed rooted in a basic sense of scarcity. The mechanism the text implies is direct: it is the mental agitation, *vihvalo*, that causes the failure of *vīrya*, strength. The temperament undermines the capacity for bold action before circumstance even gets the chance to.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Saturn in the 7th house bad for marriage?** The classical text is unflinching: even a genuinely good spouse will not feel like a source of comfort or benefit to this native.

**Does Saturn in the 7th house cause health problems for both partners?** Yes, specifically. The verse names chronic disease affecting the native and the spouse together, not one side alone.

**Does Saturn in the 7th house delay marriage?** The source text does not state a delay directly, but the isolation and lack of allies it describes are consistent with the delayed or serious-partner pattern often associated with Saturn in this house.

## Sources

*Chamatkara Chintaamani*, Shani Phalam, Verse 7, for the core results on spouse, friendship, wealth, and temperament. *Phaladeepika*, Chapter 10, for the 7th house's classical designations, *Kalabrabhaya* and *Jamitra*. *Brihat Jataka*, Chapter 1, for the anatomical mapping to the genital organ and the house's other technical synonyms.
