**Quick answer:** Rahu in the 8th house (*Randhra Bhava*, longevity and hidden resources) points to compromised longevity, a pattern of rising to real honor and then falling from it, and a conduct profile the classical sources call despicable in plain language. The texts make no attempt to soften any of it.

## What Rahu in the 8th House Means for Longevity and Health
The 8th house, *Ashtama Bhava* or *Randhra* ("the hole" or cavity), governs longevity, hidden resources, and the manner of one's exit from the world (*Phaladeepika* 8.34). Rahu's essential nature is *Tamo*, darkness, a designation fixed across the tradition and stated explicitly in the *Chamatkara Chintaamani* (Sloka 1 and 8). Darkness sitting in the hidden house obscures clarity in health, wealth, and reputation alike. The *Phaladeepika* is direct on the physical picture. It names *Alpayu*, a shortened or compromised term of life, alongside *Udara-roga*, marked susceptibility to belly and abdominal pain, and a distinct tendency toward gout or rheumatism, pointing to long-term inflammatory or circulatory trouble. Underneath all of it sits a general depletion of physical resilience.

## Rahu in the 8th House: Rise and Fall in Status
The *Chamatkara Chintaamani* (Sloka 8) describes a specific socio-economic pattern rather than uniform decline: the native is honored by kings and scholars, and then meets *Bhramsha*, a sudden fall from that elevated position. This is not one bad break. It is a structural rhythm. Three terms sharpen this picture. *Shrama-granthi*, the "knot of labor," describes effort that stays tangled or blocked, so that gains demand extreme, obstructed toil rather than arriving cleanly. Social isolation follows a similar shape: the native is at risk of being abandoned by people, which compounds into the depletion of accumulated wealth as networks fall away. And *Vraghna-shatru*, "the Sun's enemy," reflects Rahu's role as the eclipser of the Sun (vitality, authority), which in the house of death sets up both a drain on life force and friction with people in positions of power.

## Rahu in the 8th House: Conduct and Legacy
The *Phaladeepika* does not stop at circumstance. It names a tendency toward *Dusta Karma*, conduct that deviates from established ethical norms, described plainly as despicable acts. The same passage associates this placement with few children, a real limitation on sustaining a long-term lineage. Underneath both sits a native who is, in the text's words, distressed and hard-working at once, worn down by the *Shrama-granthi* named above, and generally unsettled: a life short on stability and long on restless searching for security.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Does Rahu in the 8th house shorten life span?** The classical texts name *Alpayu*, a compromised term of life, directly as one of the significations of this placement.
**Why does Rahu in the 8th house bring rise and fall rather than steady loss?** The *Chamatkara Chintaamani* describes a specific pattern of being honored by kings and scholars, then meeting a sudden fall (*Bhramsha*), rather than a flat decline.
**Does Rahu in the 8th house affect children?** Yes, the *Phaladeepika* associates this placement with having few children, limiting the continuation of the native's lineage.

## Sources
*Phaladeepika* 8.34 is the primary source for longevity, physical health, conduct, and progeny. *Chamatkara Chintaamani* (Rahu, Sloka 8) is the primary source for the rise-and-fall pattern, labor, and the Sun's-enemy dynamic. The *Brihat Jataka* establishes Rahu's general classification as *Tamo*, which frames its behavior in this house specifically.
