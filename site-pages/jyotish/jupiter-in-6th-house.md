**Quick answer:** Jupiter in the 6th house (*Shatru Bhava*, the house of enemies, debts, and disease) makes the native very hard to beat in conflict, becoming an outright destroyer of enemies, but the same strength tends to curdle into arrogance. The cost lands on the body: the native is chronically prone to disease, and the mother's health and maternal uncles' fortunes suffer too. Material stability, where it exists, comes from land and livestock rather than salaried or market wealth.

## Jupiter in the 6th House: Conflict and Personality
The 6th house is *Shatru Bhava*: enemies, debts, disease. A natural benefic sitting in this *Dushthana* (difficult house) rarely comes through clean, and the sole classical source located for this placement, the *Chamatkara Chintamani* (Guru section, Verse 6), does not try to resolve the contradiction. The verse uses *shatruhantritvam-eti*: the native becomes an outright destroyer of enemies, not someone who merely survives opposition. Whatever competitive arena this native enters, career rivalry or open conflict, they tend to walk out the other side unassailable. But the text does not let this read as pure strength. The same surplus of power is described as *valad-uddhatah*, rendered arrogant by strength, to the point that the phrase *ko rane tasya jeta* effectively asks who could possibly defeat them in battle. Guru's wisdom, funneled through a house of conflict, comes out less as humility and more as a hardened sense of superiority.

## Jupiter in the 6th House: Health
Internally, the placement is far less kind. The native is *rujarto*, chronically stricken by disease, a recurring vulnerability rather than an isolated illness. Whatever authority this person commands in the boardroom or the battlefield, it does not extend to their own physical constitution.

## Jupiter in the 6th House: Mother and Maternal Uncles
The same affliction reaches the mother: *jananya rujah*, prone to disease or chronic physical trouble. And the text is equally direct about where Jupiter's usual generosity stops. *Na tan matulanam*: the benefic effects do not extend to maternal uncles, that branch of the family getting none of the placement's grace.

## Jupiter in the 6th House: Wealth through Livestock
Material stability here is specific, and agrarian rather than urban. *Mahishya-adi-sharma*: comfort and happiness through livestock, buffaloes named explicitly, alongside other cattle-based assets. This is not a placement the text associates with market speculation or salaried prestige. The wealth, such as it is, is tied to land and livestock.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Jupiter in the 6th house good for dealing with enemies?** Yes, this is the placement's clearest strength: the classical texts describe outright victory over opposition.
**Does Jupiter in the 6th house cause health problems?** Yes, the text is explicit that the native is chronically prone to disease, in contrast to Jupiter's usual protective reputation.
**What kind of wealth does Jupiter in the 6th house bring?** Agrarian wealth specifically, tied to livestock, particularly buffaloes, rather than urban or market-based income.

## Sources
*Chamatkara Chintamani*, Guru (Jupiter) section, Verse 6, the sole classical source located for this placement, covering the conflict results, the personality effects, the affliction to mother and maternal uncles, the native's own health, and the livestock-based wealth signification.
