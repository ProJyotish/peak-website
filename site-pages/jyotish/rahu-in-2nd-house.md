**Quick answer:** Rahu in the 2nd house (*Dhana Bhava*) speaks with total, fearless conviction, not all of it true, and unsettles both family standing and wealth. Money tends to pass through the native's hands as custodian rather than owner, often under the influence of low-minded associates. The same verse names a specific risk of injury from weapons or sharp instruments.

## What Rahu in the 2nd House Means for Speech and Wealth
The source for this placement is the *Chamatkara Chintamani*, section "Dhanabhava-gata Rahu ke phal" (the fruits of Rahu in the house of wealth), Verse 2. The 2nd house, *Dhana Bhava*, governs accumulated wealth and doubles as *Kutumba Bhava* (family and lineage) and *Vachana* (speech). Rahu here carries its full technical identity: *Tamah*, the darkness, and the "Son of Simhika," a lineage that signals a predatory, unrestrained quality that "swallows" the significations of whichever house it occupies. The text names the speech pattern precisely: *Mrishabhashita*, a chronic tendency toward false or deceptive speech, paired with *Nirbhaya*, fearlessness. This is not the lying of someone covering for weakness. It is deception delivered with such unhesitating conviction that the listener struggles to locate the seam between what is true and what isn't.

On wealth, the text describes a specific paradox. The native may reach the status of *Vittapala*, literally "guardian of wealth," but this is custodianship, not ownership. It comes bundled with *Paravashyam*, a state of subjection or dependency, frequently catalyzed by association with *Khalebhyo*, wicked or low-minded people. Money gets managed for the benefit of others while the native's own resources sit under someone else's shadow.

## Rahu in the 2nd House: Family and Social Standing
The domestic cost is named directly, not implied. The text describes *nashtabhutam kutumbam*: the family unit rendered destroyed, lost, or dysfunctional. Alongside it sits *Svavargapranasho*, the loss of one's own *varga*, meaning class, kin, or social standing. This points to alienation from one's own community or lineage, whether through the native's own conduct or the outright collapse of the family's social position.

## Rahu in the 2nd House: Health and Physical Risk
One warning in this verse is worth stating plainly rather than softening: *Bhayamshastratah*, a persistent fear or danger connected to weapons (*Shastra*). Read against the 2nd house's rulership of the face, this points to real risk of injury from sharp instruments or sudden violence, not a metaphor for verbal conflict.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Rahu in the 2nd house good for wealth?** Not straightforwardly. It can bring the status of a "guardian of wealth," but the text warns of dependency and manipulation from low-minded associates rather than clean, independent ownership.
**Does Rahu in the 2nd house affect speech?** Yes, the text names a chronic pull toward false, deceptive speech delivered with unusual fearlessness.
**Is Rahu in the 2nd house bad for family relationships?** The classical text is direct about it: family standing and harmony are described as destroyed or dysfunctional under this placement.

## Sources
*Chamatkara Chintamani*, section "Dhanabhava-gata Rahu ke phal," Verse 2.
