**Quick answer:** Mars in the 3rd house (Tritiya Bhava) is one of the strongest placements for Mangal on the whole list, since Mars is the natural *Karaka* (significator) of this house. It gives real physical courage and wealth earned through personal effort, but the same fire brings distress to siblings and unexpected social ridicule for any spiritual practice the native takes up.

## What Mars in the 3rd House Means for Courage and Physical Strength
The primary source is a single verse from the *Chamatkara Chintamani* by Bhatta Narayana (Bhauma-phala, Verse 3), structured as a rhetorical chain: if Mars is not in the third house, from where would *Bahu-viryam* (strength of arms) come for human beings? The 3rd house is an *Upachaya Bhava*, a house of growth where malefic planets actually help, and Mars is its *Karaka*, so this placement is structurally favorable. The result is *Parakrama*, an aggressive bravery that isn't performative: an athletic or physically capable build, and a native who simply isn't cowed by opposition.

## Mars in the 3rd House: Wealth and Career
The same verse names *Bahu-lakshmi*, great wealth, as dependent on this placement: from where else would abundant wealth arise for the sons of men? This wealth is explicitly earned through *Parakrama*, personal exertion and enterprise, not inheritance or ease.

## Mars in the 3rd House: Effects on Siblings
The same fire that builds the native's own strength burns the people connected to that house. *Sahottha-vyatha*, distress originating from siblings, is the direct cost the verse names. The *Phaladeepika* (Chapter 2) classifies Mars as a *Krura* (cruel) planet, and the mechanism is a standard one in Jyotish logic: a malefic well-placed for the native's own agency will still damage the living relationships that house represents. Expect friction with brothers and sisters, their ill health, or a burden they place on the native rather than the reverse.

## Mars in the 3rd House: Spiritual Life and Reputation
The native may genuinely have the discipline for *Tapaścaryā* (spiritual austerity), but the *rajasic*, aggressive quality of Mars means these efforts read to others as ostentatious or insincere. *Upahāsyaḥ*, ridicule, is the specific word the *Chamatkara Chintamani* uses. The *Brihat Samhita* (Chapter 6, *Bhauma-cara*) details the heat and aggression of Mars that underlies this social mockery.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Mars in the 3rd house good or bad?** Largely good. Mars is the natural significator of this house, and the placement builds real courage and self-earned wealth, though it costs the native something in sibling relationships.
**Does Mars in the 3rd house affect siblings?** Yes, the text names *Sahottha-vyatha*, distress from siblings, directly as the cost of this placement.
**Does Mars in the 3rd house bring wealth?** Yes, *Bahu-lakshmi*, wealth earned through personal exertion rather than inheritance.

## Sources
*Chamatkara Chintamani* by Bhatta Narayana, Bhauma-phala, Verse 3. *Phaladeepika* by Mantreswara, Chapter 2. *Brihat Jataka* of Varaha Mihira, Chapter 1. *Brihat Samhita* by Varaha Mihira, Chapter 6 (*Bhauma-cara*).
