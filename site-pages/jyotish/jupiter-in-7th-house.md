**Quick answer:** Jupiter in the 7th house (*Jamitra*, the house of partnership) gives a breadth of mind and wealth that flows through alliances, an excellent spouse, and success in ethical trade, all channeled through what the texts call *Akhanda Kandarpa*, undisrupted vitality. The one real risk is degree-based: Jupiter caught in a specific junctional zone, the *Riksha sandhi*, can see that stability fracture regardless of the house placement.

## Jupiter in the 7th House: Personality and Wealth
The 7th house, *Jamitra*, is where the *Brihat Jataka* (Chapter 1, Stanza 4) locates the genital organ of the *Kalapurusha*, the cosmic body the chart maps onto. It is the seat of *Kama*, desire, governing marriage, commerce, and travel in the same breath. Jupiter's presence does not suppress that raw signification, it refines it. The *Chamatkara Chintamani* (Guru, Verse 7) leans hard on abundance, using variants of *bahu* (many, great) repeatedly. *Matih tasya bahni*: a mind of real breadth, able to navigate complex social and legal terrain with genuine wisdom. *Vibhutis cha vahni*: significant, manifold wealth, typically arriving as a byproduct of the native's social standing and the quality of their alliances rather than direct labor.

## Jupiter in the 7th House: Marriage and Vitality
The verse describes *rati* (pleasure) with excellent partners and reiterates *Akhanda Kandarpa*, unbroken vitality, consistent with the *Brihat Jataka*'s framing of the 7th as the seat of the generative organ, here sustained by Jupiter's *Jiva* (life-giving) essence. Marriage functions as a genuine source of status and joy rather than an obligation, and the native's drive toward an "undisrupted" union tends to spill outward into a rich, active social life more broadly.

## Jupiter in the 7th House: Career and Trade
The 7th house governs the merchant with the scales (*Juka*) and the traveler, and Jupiter here supports success in foreign transactions and business conducted on ethical, scholarly terms rather than sharp practice. The native, in other words, tends to prosper through who they are connected to as much as through what they personally produce, and the *Phaladeepika* (Chapter 10, index) confirms the 7th as *Kalatrabhava*, the house of the spouse, tying the marital and commercial themes together.

## Jupiter in the 7th House: The Riksha Sandhi Caution
The classical caveat here is technical but important. *Brihat Jataka* (Stanza 9) requires a planet's strength to be evaluated across six divisions, the *Shadvarga*: *Drekkana, Hora, Navamsa, Trimsamsa, Dwadasamsa*, and *Kshetra*. If Jupiter holds its own *Varga* in the 7th, the prosperity and intellectual strength described above turn stable and enduring. But Stanza 7 of the same text names a specific danger zone: the final *Navamsas* of Cancer, Scorpio, and Pisces are *Riksha sandhis*, junctional points where a planet loses cohesive strength. Jupiter caught in a *Riksha sandhi*, even while occupying the 7th house, can see the "unbroken" nature of its promised vitality and fortune fracture instead, showing up as instability in partnership or sudden shifts in material standing. Where Jupiter instead falls in signs like Aries or Taurus, the emphasis shifts toward trade-related travel and physical vitality rather than the domestic and intellectual themes above.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Jupiter in the 7th house good for marriage?** Yes, generally: the texts describe an excellent spouse and marriage as a genuine source of status and joy.
**Is Jupiter in the 7th house good for business or trade?** Yes, particularly foreign transactions and business conducted on ethical, scholarly terms.
**What can weaken Jupiter in the 7th house?** A degree-based condition called Riksha sandhi, where Jupiter falls in the final degrees of Cancer, Scorpio, or Pisces, which can fracture the placement's usual stability.

## Sources
*Chamatkara Chintamani*, Guru section, Verse 7 (core results on intellect, wealth, marriage, and vitality). *Brihat Jataka*, Chapter 1, Stanzas 4, 5, 7, and 9 (the 7th house as *Jamitra*, Jupiter's lordship, the *Riksha sandhi* warning, and the *Shadvarga* framework). *Phaladeepika* (Chapter 10, index) confirms the 7th as *Kalatrabhava*, the house of the spouse.
