**Quick answer:** Moon in the 6th house (Ripu Bhava, the house of enemies and disease) gives repeated victory over rivals and resilient health, but the classical texts are explicit that this comes at the cost of any real internal quiet. The native rules "like a king" specifically amid conflict, per *Chamatkara Chintaamani*, which frames this as dominance over peace, not dominance and peace.

## Moon in the 6th House: Victory Over Enemies
Chandra rules Kataka (Cancer) and is defined, per *Brihat Jataka* (Chapter 1, Stanza 1), as *Surja rasmischandramah*, the rays of the Sun. *Chamatkara Chintaamani* settles on *Ripau*, the house of enemies, as the operative name for the 6th, and its Chandra Phala (Verse 6) describes the native as *Rajate... Raja*, shining and ruling like a king, specifically amid *Vigrahena*, strife or dispute. Opponents are rendered *Arayo-nishprabha*, literally lusterless, a description read as much psychological as literal. The text adds *Bhuyo*, repeatedly, to describe how often this happens: not a single victory, a pattern.

## Moon in the 6th House: Temperament
The valor here is *Pratapojwalo*, blazing, radiant, visible: a warrior's strength built on clarity and light overwhelming darkness, not a hermit's quiet strength. A specific detail worth taking seriously: the text names *Matrushila*, a maternal disposition, as the actual blueprint for how the native manages conflict. Whatever the mother's own character or struggles were, that inheritance shapes how this native fights and protects.

## Moon in the 6th House: Health
The 6th is traditionally the house of *Roga*, disease, and here vitality depends on the Moon's ability to keep its reflected light "unaffected" by strife. Framed plainly: so long as the native's *Pratap* stays bright, the body holds up. Victory over illness is described in the same terms as victory over rivals, a matter of clarity and light overcoming the house's native darkness.

## Moon in the 6th House: Dominance Without Peace
This is the summary judgment the source itself reaches, not a footnote: an auspicious placement for worldly power, and it denies the native internal quietude. The *Ripau* house does not stop being the house of conflict just because the native usually wins in it. Constant activity is demanded, not offered as an option, and the text does not suggest the native gets to opt out of the fight to enjoy the winnings.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Moon in the 6th house good or bad?** Good for worldly power and victory over rivals, but the text is explicit that it costs the native inner peace.
**Does Moon in the 6th house affect health?** Yes, health here is tied directly to the native's ability to stay "radiant" under conflict.
**Does Moon in the 6th house bring victory over enemies?** Yes, repeatedly, per the term *Bhuyo* used in *Chamatkara Chintaamani*.

## Sources
*Chamatkara Chintaamani*, Chandra Phala, Verse 6. *Brihat Jataka*, Chapter 1, Stanza 1, Stanza 3, and Stanza 6.
