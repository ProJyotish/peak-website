**Quick answer:** Sun in the 3rd house (Tritiya Bhava, the house of courage and self-effort) gives real majesty (*Pratapadhikam*) and valor (*Vikramam*), a consistent record of subduing enemies, royal or state favor won specifically through conflict rather than diplomacy, and a pull toward pilgrimage. The cost lands on the siblings, who absorb the heat this Sun generates everywhere else.

## What Sun in the 3rd House Means for Temperament
The source is the *Chamatkara Chintaamani* (Surya Phalam, Verse 3), which is unusually direct about this placement: the native is meant to expand outward, not hold still. Two Sanskrit terms do the work, and they aren't synonyms even though they're often flattened into one. *Pratapadhikam* is the external glow, the aura of majesty that makes a native visible and commands recognition without having to ask for it. *Vikramam* is the internal supply: valor, physical courage, the willingness to actually do the difficult thing rather than just look capable of it. The verb the text uses for the overall effect is *atanoti*, to spread or stretch outward. This is not a native content to hold ground; the signature is expansion, in territory, in reputation, in whatever the native decides is worth pursuing.

## Sun in the 3rd House: Conflict and Authority
The 3rd house rewards this kind of Sun. *Sadorshikshayah* names a native who consistently subdues or disciplines opponents, a pattern rather than an occasional win. Favor from the state or sovereign arrives through a specific channel: *Sangare sham nareshat*, peace and benefit from the king specifically in the context of battle. Not diplomacy, not quiet favor-currying. **Respect here is earned by demonstrating prowess under pressure, not by staying out of the fight.** The same *Pratapadhikam* and *Vikramam* that define the native's temperament are exactly what the sovereign ends up rewarding.

## Sun in the 3rd House: Siblings
The siblings absorb what the rest of the chart doesn't. *Sodaraistapyate*: the native is heated, pained, by brothers and sisters. The same solar force that builds authority and conquers opposition in the world runs too hot for the sibling bond specifically. This isn't a footnote; the text names it in the same breath as the majesty and the valor. Whatever this placement builds outward, it costs something inward, in exactly the relationship that is supposed to be the uncomplicated one.

## Sun in the 3rd House: Pilgrimage and Spirituality
The 3rd house's native restlessness, combined with the Sun's pull toward truth and duty, produces the *Tirthachari*, one who wanders specifically to holy sites. It is not idle travel. It is *Vikramam* applied to the spiritual project instead of the worldly one, physical energy spent seeking out *Tirthas* as a form of religious obligation. Even the devotion here has the shape of an expedition rather than a retreat.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Sun in the 3rd house good for career and authority?** Yes, this is one of the stronger placements for the Sun: it consistently supports subduing rivals and earning favor from those in power.
**Does Sun in the 3rd house affect siblings?** Yes, the text names sibling friction directly (*Sodaraistapyate*), even as the native's own standing rises.
**Does Sun in the 3rd house support spiritual practice?** Yes, it inclines the native toward pilgrimage and holy travel (*Tirthachari*) rather than idle wandering.

## Sources
*Chamatkara Chintaamani*, Surya Phalam, Verse 3, for personality (*Pratapadhikam*, *Vikramam*, *atanoti*), siblings (*Sodaraistapyate*), enemies and sovereign favor (*Sadorshikshayah*, *Sangare sham nareshat*), and pilgrimage (*Tirthachari*).
