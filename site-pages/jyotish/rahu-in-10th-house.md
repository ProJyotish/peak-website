**Quick answer:** Rahu in the 10th house (*Karma Bhava*, career and status) is traditionally well-placed for worldly achievement, since the 10th is an *Upachaya* house. The rise is real, but the texts are careful to specify it comes through unconventional associations and ruthless tactics rather than sanctioned means, and the native pays for it in lost sleep and restless pride.

## What Rahu in the 10th House Means for Career and Ambition
The 10th house, *Karma Bhava*, is an *Upachaya* house, one where a malefic like Rahu is traditionally considered well-placed for worldly achievement. The *Chamatkara Chintamani* (Rahu Section, Verse 10) ties the native's professional rise to *mlecchasamsargato*, associations with foreigners, outsiders, or people beyond the traditional social fold. The professional conduct itself is named *krurakarma*, cruel or ruthless action: a willingness to use aggressive, morally flexible tactics to secure power, driven by ego and pride rather than convention. Beyond the natal picture, the *Brihat Samhita* (Chapter 5, Rahu-cara) describes Rahu's broader orbital course in terms of terrestrial upheaval, which in a personal chart manifests as real volatility in career status rather than a steady climb. The *Phaladeepika* (Chapter 8) situates this within the general logic of *Upachaya* houses: malefics placed here drive the native toward material success, but the cost is paid personally, not waived.

## Rahu in the 10th House: Personality and Pride
Three descriptors define the psychological cost of this success. *Ativagarvam* is intense, excessive pride, a haughty professional bearing that comes with visible rank. *Janairvyakulo* cuts against that visibility directly: despite the standing, the native is persistently troubled and agitated by the very people they deal with. And *sukham nadhishete* is the plainest of the three, the native finds no happiness or restful sleep, a mind worn down by the weight of *krurakarma* and the unrelenting pressure of ambition.

## Rahu in the 10th House: Relationships, Wealth, and the Lunar Contrast
The native pursues *bhogamucchaih*, the height of sensory pleasure and worldly luxury, largely as confirmation of status. In relationships, there is a pull toward *manini kamini*, partners who are proud, attractive, or of high social standing, associations that tend to reflect the native's own professional power back at them. Spending follows the same logic: *madarthavyayi* describes wealth spent for pride, vanity, or "intoxicants," a phrase the tradition extends past substances to the addictive pull of power and status itself. The text draws its own comparison to the Moon in the same house, which produces *samudrangajese*, wealth from the sea and royal favor, alongside real domestic and mental peace. Rahu replaces that stability with *krurakarma* and *mlecchasamsargato*, and where the Moon offers rest, Rahu leaves the native *sukham nadhishete*, unable to find it.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Rahu in the 10th house good for career?** Yes, in the classical sense of worldly achievement, since the 10th is an *Upachaya* house. The success arrives through unconventional or ruthless means rather than clean, traditional channels.
**Does Rahu in the 10th house affect sleep and peace of mind?** Yes, the text names *sukham nadhishete* directly, meaning the native finds no restful sleep even at high status.
**How does Rahu in the 10th house differ from the Moon in the 10th house?** The text contrasts the two directly: the Moon offers domestic and mental peace with royal favor, while Rahu offers status through ruthless action without that peace.

## Sources
