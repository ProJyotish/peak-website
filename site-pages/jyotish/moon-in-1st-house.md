**Quick answer:** Moon in the 1st house (Lagna, the house of the body and self) gives beauty and a settled, contented mind when the Moon is strong, but poverty, physical frailty, and mental dullness when it is weak or waning. It also tends to quietly obstruct other wealth-forming yogas in the chart. Unusually, classical exaltation logic is reversed here: the *Bhavartha Ratnakara* calls the native fortunate in any sign except Taurus (Vrishabha), the Moon's own sign of exaltation.

## What Moon in the 1st House Means for Personality and Body
The primary source is Bhatta Narayana's *Chamatkara Chintamani*, and it splits this placement cleanly by the Moon's strength. A strong Moon, described with the dignity term *Gokulirajagah* (strength in Vrishabha or its own sign), produces a *Dhanadhyaksha*, literally "lord of wealth," along with *Lavanya*, a luminous and charming physical form, and *Anandapurnam*, an enduring fullness and joy (*Chamatkara Chintamani*, 1st House, Verse 1). A weak or waning (*Ksheena*) Moon flips this: *Daridram* (poverty), *Ksheenadeham* (physical frailty), and *Jadam Shrotrahinam*, mental dullness paired with diminished hearing or perceptive sharpness, are all named directly.

## Moon in the 1st House: Effects on Wealth
Here the texts complicate each other. *Chamatkara Chintamani* calls a strong 1st-house Moon a "lord of wealth." But the *Bhavartha Ratnakara* adds a separate, more technical claim: a Moon in the Lagna, even a reasonably strong one, tends to act as a quiet spoiler on other *Dhana Yogas* elsewhere in the chart, getting in the way of the native fully realizing favorable wealth combinations (*Bhavartha Ratnakara*, Chapter 1, Stanza 14). Both claims come from serious sources, and a careful reading has to hold both rather than pick one.

## Moon in the 1st House and Mental Temperament
Because the Lagna represents the physical self, the Moon's placement here dictates the native's baseline emotional weather more directly than in most houses. A strong Moon gives *Anandapurnam*, contentment that holds steady rather than mood that swings. Chandra's link to *Varna*, the native's complexion and social grace, extends this: when the Moon is well placed, the personality itself reads as warm and settled, not just the face.

## Moon in the 1st House in Taurus (Vrishabha)
Standard exaltation logic says Chandra in Vrishabha should be the strongest possible version of this placement. The *Bhavartha Ratnakara* disagrees directly: the native is called "very fortunate" if the Moon occupies the Lagna in any sign **except** Vrishabha (*Bhavartha Ratnakara*, Chapter 1, Stanza 15). Exaltation, in this one specific case, is named as a hindrance to fortune rather than its peak expression, and the text does not soften or explain away the contradiction.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Moon in the 1st house good or bad?** Neither outright. It reliably gives beauty and vitality, but wealth depends heavily on the Moon's strength and sign.
**Does Moon in the 1st house affect wealth?** Yes. A strong Moon is called a "lord of wealth," but the same placement can obstruct other Dhana Yogas in the chart.
**Is Moon exalted in the 1st house good?** Not according to the *Bhavartha Ratnakara*, which names every sign except Taurus as the fortunate version of this placement.
