**Quick answer:** Jupiter in the 1st house (Tanu Bhava, the house of self and body) gives a dignified, gracious presence: the native is *Devapujya*, held in high regard even by those of elevated consciousness, with genuine virtue and a naturally happy disposition. The one real caveat is *Alpa-veerya*, reduced physical stamina, so the presence reads stronger than the muscle behind it. Wealth gets directed toward noble causes rather than hoarded, and specific chart combinations involving the Moon and Jupiter-Mercury in Pisces can trigger a strong Dhana Yoga.

## What Jupiter in the 1st House Means for Personality and Body
The primary source is the *Chamatkara Chintamani* (Jupiter, 1st House, Verse 1). It names the native *Devapujya*: worshiped, or held in highest esteem, by the Devas themselves, a claim the text does not hedge. The body is *Divya-deho*, a "divine" frame carrying dignity, beauty, and a commanding presence that registers before the person speaks. *Gunitvam*, virtue as an inborn trait rather than a performance, rounds this out: the native is *Sukhi* (fundamentally happy) and *Suveshi* (carries themselves and dresses with care).

**Here is the one place the text pulls back.** Alongside the divine body, the same verse notes *Alpa-veerya*: reduced physical potency and raw stamina. The frame is refined, not built for manual labor or sustained physical exertion. A placement this favorable still comes with a stated limit.

## Jupiter in the 1st House: Wealth and Career
The verse states *"Vasuni vyayam sambalena vrajanti"*: wealth moves toward strong, noble causes, temples, education, community welfare, rather than personal indulgence. This is not a native who hoards. *Gunitvam* extends naturally into a career built on counsel and leadership rather than raw ambition, a reputation for wisdom that other people find genuinely useful to have in the room.

## Jupiter in the 1st House: Spirituality and Health
*Gatir-bhavini paraloki*: a high standing in the afterlife, a spiritual trajectory that outlasts this one life. Read next to *Alpa-veerya*, the pattern holds together: the native's vital energy was never allocated toward gross physical endurance, but toward the refinement of the spirit instead. The *Shastras* stay largely quiet on disease for this placement, which a scholar reads as Brihaspati protecting the *Vapu* (physical frame) rather than as an absence of information, though the stamina caveat still stands.

## Dasha Windows for Jupiter in the 1st House
The *Bhavartha Ratnakara* ties specific outcomes to specific chart configurations, not to Jupiter in the *Lagna* alone. Mars and Venus in the *Lagna* while Jupiter sits in Capricorn: the Mercury and Jupiter *Dashas* are marked exceptionally fortunate (BR Ch. 12). The Moon in the *Lagna*, Taurus excepted: the native becomes "very fortunate," though the Moon here generally afflicts other *Dhana Yogas* even as it grants this specific fortune (BR Ch. 1, 12). Moon and Venus in Libra, with Jupiter and Mercury in Pisces: the Jupiter *Dasha* triggers a strong *Dhana Yoga*, and the Venus *Dasha* brings, in the text's own words, "lots of wealth."

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is Jupiter in the 1st house good or bad?** It is one of Jupiter's more favorable placements: a dignified, virtuous presence, with reduced physical stamina as the main caveat.
**Does Jupiter in the 1st house give wealth?** Yes, though the texts frame it as wealth used for noble purposes rather than personal accumulation, with specific Dasha combinations triggering stronger Dhana Yoga.
**What health issues does Jupiter in the 1st house cause?** The classical texts are largely silent on disease here, read as protective, but flag reduced physical stamina (*Alpa-veerya*) as the placement's one limit.
